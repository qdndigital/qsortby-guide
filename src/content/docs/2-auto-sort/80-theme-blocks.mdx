Most stores need **none of these**. A sorted collection needs no theme work: the app writes the order back to Shopify and your theme renders it like any other collection. Blocks are for placing a feed somewhere Shopify has no collection — a row on the home page, a rail under the add-to-cart button — and there are four of them, each a block you drop into any theme section from the theme editor.

| Block | What it renders | Usual home |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **For You** | A different order per visitor, driven by a [Personalized collection](/docs/how-it-works/ranking-personalize) and your published rule | Home page, collection page top |
| **Best sellers** | One shop-wide order over a sales window set on the block itself | Home page row |
| **Goes with** | Partners from your [Goes-with rules](/docs/goes-with/goes-with-overview) for the product being viewed | Product page |
| **Frequently bought together** | Real co-purchase partners for the product being viewed — the [FBT upsell](/docs/upsell/bought-together) | Product page, under add-to-cart |

The app reads your live theme to tell you whether a block is placed: the **Personalization** tab shows it for the For You block, and the bought-together upsell's editor for the Frequently bought together block. A block that is not placed is the single most common reason a surface reports zero views: it has nowhere to render. And you probably do not need all four — check whether a collection page already does the job first.

<Callout type="warning" title="The app embed comes first">
Every block renders through the QSortby theme app embed. If the embed is switched off, all four show nothing, whatever their settings — Home's Health panel flags it, and [Setup](/docs/getting-started/setup) step 1 is where to fix it.
</Callout>

## Adding a block

Theme editor → add a section or block → pick the QSortby block (they are listed as **Algorithm - For You**, **Algorithm - Best Sellers**, **Algorithm - Goes With** and **Algorithm - Bought With**). All four live in the same place and share most of their layout settings. The two feeds — For You and Best sellers — have the settings below; Goes with and Frequently bought together take theirs from the rule or the offer that feeds them.

For the **For You** block, the one setting that matters most is **the collection it points at** (**Personalize collection**). The dropdown lists every collection in your shop, but the block only fetches a personalized feed when the collection you pick is a **Personalize** collection. Leave it blank and the block falls back to your shop-wide For You settings.

The **Best Sellers** block needs no collection: set **Rolling window (days)** (1–90, default 7) and **Total products to load** (default 60) and it ranks your catalogue directly.

## Three things a block can do when something's off

Worth knowing, because the three look different on the storefront and mean different things:

**Shop-wide For You is switched off.** The block renders nothing and clears its placeholder. That old shop-wide switch is no longer on any settings screen, so you are unlikely to meet this one.

**The collection no longer exists.** The block shows a message aimed at you, not shoppers: *"The collection set on this block doesn't exist anymore. Pick another in the theme editor."* This happens when a collection is deleted in Shopify after the block was configured — the block keeps the old handle until you change it.

**The collection isn't a Personalize collection.** This one is deliberately *not* treated as an error. If you've pointed the block at one of your theme's product snippets, it renders that collection's own product order instead — so shoppers still see products, just not personalized ones. Without a snippet configured the block clears silently rather than showing an empty state, because there's nothing wrong with the collection, only with the block pointing at it.

<Callout type="tip" title="Check the browser console while you configure">
The For You block logs which visitor group a shopper matched, the numbers behind that decision and how many products came back. If a feed looks wrong, that line usually says why before you have to guess.
</Callout>

## Pagination and the per-visitor cache

The feed is paginated **on the server**. The block asks for one page at a time and QSortby ranks the whole list once, caches it for that visitor, and slices to the page asked for — so Prev/Next and infinite scroll are near-instant instead of re-running the ranker.

Two settings control the shape of that:

- **Products per page** — how many cards are visible at once.
- **Pagination style** — numbered **Prev/Next** controls, or **infinite scroll** that loads the next page as the shopper nears the bottom.

And two separate caches control the freshness:

- **Refresh interval** on the collection — how long a single visitor's ranked list is reused before QSortby recomputes it. **1 hour by default**, tunable from 1 to 24. Up to 100 products are held per visitor.
- **Cache duration (minutes)** on the block — how long the visitor's own browser reuses the previous render on reload before asking again. Default 5 minutes; 0 always fetches.

<Callout type="warning" title="Collection settings wait for the cache; a published rule does not">
Publishing a different rule, or saving an edit to the live one, starts a fresh ranking on each shopper's next load. A change to the collection's own settings below does not: a shopper who already has a feed built keeps their old order until the **Refresh interval** expires — up to 24 hours if you've raised it. Keep the interval short while you're iterating.
</Callout>

The cache is keyed per shopper, per collection and per cart, so a shopper who adds something to their cart gets a fresh ranking straight away rather than waiting out the interval.

## Per-collection settings worth knowing

A Personalize collection carries its own copy of the knobs the feed uses, so two feeds in the same shop can behave differently:

- **Products shown** — how many products a visitor sees, across every page (1–50, default 24).
- **Intent suggest** — the on/off for the [intent read](/docs/upsell/intent-suggest) on this feed.
- **Refresh interval (hours)** — as above.
- **Return rate (days) / Session (minutes)** — how far back those two signals look. (The return-rate signal currently scores every product the same — a known bug — so its window changes nothing yet.)
- **Hide poorly rated products**, with a star threshold and a minimum review count.
- **Leave out products already in the cart** — off by default.
- **Mix up the first ten for 1 in 20 views** — lets Intent AI learn from shoppers' clicks.
- **Family** — restrict the feed to one category.

Products you've excluded shop-wide in [Settings](/docs/settings/settings-overview) are dropped from every feed before it's cached, so a cached list never holds a product that was excluded when it was built. A list cached before you excluded something keeps it until the **Refresh interval** expires.

## Appearance

Both blocks share the same layout vocabulary: columns and gap per breakpoint, padding and margin per breakpoint, heading text, alignment, colour, weight and size per breakpoint, and an optional **container class** so the block picks up your theme's own width wrapper (`page-width` on Dawn, `container` on many others).

For cards that match your storefront exactly, point the block at one of your theme's own product snippets instead of using QSortby's card markup — the same **App cards vs Theme cards** choice the [upsell widgets](/docs/upsell/cart-upsell) offer.
