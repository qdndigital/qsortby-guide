Click any row on **Auto-sort → Products** to open a single product. The metrics at the top are the same ones from the list; the card underneath answers a different question: **what does this product travel with, and on what basis?** The card is titled **What it travels with**. Its **Which relationship** filter opens on **All** — each partner once, under the strongest relationship it has — and offers five full lists: **Bought**, **Added**, **Viewed**, **Similar** and **Goes with**, each with its count.

Three of the lists report what shoppers did. Two do not: one is what you configured, one is inferred. They are kept apart on purpose.

## What shoppers actually did

| List | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| **Bought** | Products that appear in the same completed orders |
| **Added** | Products added to the same cart, whether or not it converted |
| **Viewed** | Products seen in the same browsing session |

These are observations from your own store. Nothing here is a setting, and nothing here was chosen by you — which is exactly what makes them useful when you are deciding what to configure.

## What you configured

**Goes with** shows the Goes-with rule this product falls under, if any — the rule that keeps a set adjacent in a sorted collection and fills the Goes-with rail on its page. See [Goes with](/docs/goes-with/goes-with-overview).

<Callout type="note" title="Evidence and configuration stay separate">
A product set you wrote will never appear under Bought, and co-purchase data will never quietly become a rule. If your own rule showed up as evidence, you would be reading your decision back to yourself as proof that the decision was right.
</Callout>

## Similar — and how each row was scored

**Similar** is the one list that is inferred rather than observed: products the catalogue implies are alike. Each row shows **How alike** as a percentage, and how many of its signals had a reading. The score is built from:

- **Same product type** · **Same vendor** — structural matches
- **Shared tags** · **Shared collections** — how you have organised the catalogue
- **Similar price** — closeness in price band
- **Sells well** — general popularity, a tie-breaker

So a match you disagree with is never a dead end. Look at what the two products share on that list and go fix the data — retag the product, move it out of a collection — rather than concluding the feature is broken.

Two details worth knowing:

- **A signal that could not be compared is left out, not scored as 0.** If neither product has tags, tags simply do not count toward the score, and the row says fewer signals had a reading. Zero would read as "no overlap" when the truth is "nothing to compare".
- **Co-purchase is reported here but kept out of the score.** If two products genuinely sell together you will see *bought together 12 times* beside the row — labelled, and never folded into the similarity number, because "bought with" and "alike" are different questions.
