A **recipe** is a ranking formula with a name. It decides **order only** — never prices, never products, never which collection something belongs to. The pickers name them in different ways: [Setup](/docs/getting-started/setup) offers three (Best selling, Trending now, New & selling); **Create collection** and **Apply a recipe** list the criteria behind them by name; a collection's **Sort products by** offers Best selling, the engagement metrics and Top rated. There, a recipe that has no data to rank by on your store is marked — *no data yet*, *collecting* or *still importing* — rather than letting it look ready.

| Recipe | What leads | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Best selling** | Most units sold in the window, sold-out products pushed to the end. The safe default. | Orders |
| **Trending now** | Rate of change, not total — what is climbing this week. A sales floor stops a jump from 1 to 3 units winning. | Orders in two consecutive windows |
| **New & selling** | Products launched inside the window, newest first (the *Newest* criterion). Needs no sales history. | Launch dates |
| **Profit first** | Highest margin first (the *Most profitable* criterion). Products with no margin are left out of the ranking. | A **cost per item** on your Shopify variants |
| **Personalized** | A different order per shopper, driven by your [personalization rule](/docs/personalization/create-a-rule). | The For You theme block, and a published rule |
| **Write your own** | One engagement metric over its own window (*Your own formula*). No picker offers it any more; a collection already set to it keeps running it. | Pixel traffic |

## Best seller and trending are different questions

A product holding at 168 units a month is a **best seller**. A product that went from 4 to 11 units this week is **trending**. Both are worth showing, and they need different recipes — which is why the [Products tab](/docs/auto-sort/products-tab) sits next to the collection list rather than in Results: it is where you find out which question a collection should answer before you commit it to one. *New Arrivals* is the usual home for Trending now, re-sorting hourly.

## The sales floor

Trending now carries a **sales floor**, and it damps rather than excludes. A product whose sales in the window sit well below the best seller's — under about 15% of it — keeps its rank but has its trend score multiplied down, so it can still appear, just not lead on a jump from one unit to three. A hard cutoff would hide a genuine riser in its first week; the floor only stops it winning before it has earned it. The floor is part of the recipe, not a setting.

## The twelve criteria

Behind the recipes are twelve **criteria**. **Create collection** lists them grouped by kind, so a shop can hold a collection on a criterion no recipe name covers — *Top revenue*, say, or *Selling fast* — and the collection list names what each one actually runs:

Best selling · Top revenue · Most profitable · Customer favorites · Trending · Trending now · Selling fast · Newest · Top rated · Engagement (most impressions, most clicks, highest CTR, most added to cart, longest dwell, best converting) · Personalize · Your own formula.

Most have their own page under [How ranking works](/docs/how-it-works/ranking-best-selling), with the arithmetic and the window it reads.

<Callout type="note" title="Shopify has a sort called “Best selling” too — it is a different order">
Shopify's own collection sort dropdown offers *Best selling*: an opaque all-time figure with no stock handling. QSortby's is units sold in the collection's rolling window with sold-out products demoted. The two extra words — "in your window", "sold-out last" — are the two differences, and they are why a collection sorted by QSortby is set to **Manual** in Shopify rather than to Shopify's Best selling.
</Callout>

## Two recipes that look ready and are not

**Profit first** needs a cost per item on your Shopify variants. Without it the recipe has nothing to rank by: a product with no cost is left out of the ranking, so on a shop with no costs there is nothing for it to put first.

**Top rated** needs reviews, from a review app QSortby can read (Judge.me, Fera, REVIEWS.io, Stamped and others; **Settings → Integrations** shows which source it found). Uninstalling a review app does not delete its metafields, so a shop that switched apps can keep ranking on the dead app's frozen numbers — if your ratings have stopped moving, pin the live source there.
