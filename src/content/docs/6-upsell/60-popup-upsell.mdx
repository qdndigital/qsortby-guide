The popup is the only surface that can appear on **any** page, which makes it the most powerful and the easiest to overuse. It's built to be gated: a trigger decides *when*, and a [display rule](/docs/upsell/display-rules) decides *whether*.

Included from **Pro**, together with the rule engine.

## Pick a trigger

- **Add to cart** — fires just after a shopper adds something. The most natural moment: they've committed, and you're suggesting a companion.
- **Exit intent** — fires when the pointer heads for the browser chrome. Desktop only by nature.
- **Cart value** — fires once the cart crosses an amount you set. Good for pairing with free shipping.
- **Time on page** — after a delay you choose.
- **Scroll depth** — after a percentage of the page.

## Keep it from becoming noise

Two settings do most of the work here, and the defaults are deliberately quiet:

- **Times per session** — how often one shopper can see it in a single visit. Default 1.
- **Cooldown (hours)** — how long before a shopper who's already seen it can see it again. Default 24.

<Callout type="warning" title="A popup with no rule shows to everyone">
Rules are opt-in. A popup with none attached fires for every shopper who hits its trigger. On a page-wide surface that's rarely what you want — attach a rule so it fires for the carts that need the nudge.
</Callout>

## Appearance

**Layout** is a centred **modal** or a corner **slide-in**; **size** sets the panel width. Then the usual grid or slider, columns, card style, and heading — or point it at one of your theme's product snippets so the cards match your storefront exactly.

## What it suggests

Same choices as the cart widget: **Rank by** and a **window**, or **Manual** with a hand-picked list. Products in the cart and out-of-stock products are excluded.

## The gate is re-checked when it fires

A popup is armed when the page loads but its rule is evaluated **when it actually fires**, against the cart as it stands at that moment. Carts change between arming and firing — a shopper removes an item, or crosses your threshold — and a check made at page load would already be stale. QSortby re-checks on the server, so a rule can't be bypassed from the browser.

If the rule no longer holds, nothing shows. That's the intended outcome, not a failure.
