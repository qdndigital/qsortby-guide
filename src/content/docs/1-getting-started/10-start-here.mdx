QSortby decides **what order your products appear in**, and keeps that order up to date without anyone touching it. You pick a recipe — "Best selling", say — and QSortby re-sorts the collection on a schedule and whenever a new order lands, so your best and fastest-moving products stay at the front and sold-out ones drop to the back.

Once that is earning, it can also **suggest one more product** — in the cart, on the product page, after checkout. That is the second half of the app, and it is deliberately the second thing you set up.

Everything QSortby does is readable: the recipe is yours to see and change, every sort saves the order it produced, and the revenue figure says plainly what it measures. There is no hidden model deciding your storefront.

## The seven places in the app

The left-hand menu has seven entries, and that is the whole menu. Each answers one question; everything else is reached by clicking a row, a button or a link inside one of them.

| **Home** | Is it making money? | [Home](/docs/getting-started/home) |
| **Auto-sort** | What order do products appear in? Collections, the Products tab, Personalization and Goes with live here. | [Auto-sort a collection](/docs/auto-sort/auto-sort-a-collection) |

<Callout type="note" title="If you used QSortby before autumn 2026">
The menu used to have eleven entries. Four of them — Products, Goes with, Personalize, Analytics — were views of something you already had open, so they now live as tabs inside **Auto-sort** and **Results**. "Merchandising" is called **Auto-sort**, "Analytics" is **Results**, and the layer once called "Emotional" is **Intent**. Nothing you configured has moved or changed; only the names and the doors.
</Callout>

## A sensible first hour

1. Finish [Setup](/docs/getting-started/setup): switch the app embed on in your theme and auto-sort one collection you already have. Setup ends when a collection on your storefront is actually re-ordered — not on a congratulations screen.
2. Open [Home](/docs/getting-started/home) a day later. The one number worth judging the app on is **influenced revenue**, and it needs orders to exist first.
3. Read [Your first 10 days](/docs/getting-started/reverse-trial) before you build anything else, because what you see on day one is the Growth plan, not the plan you'll settle onto.
4. Then pick one more thing: a second recipe on another collection, a [Goes-with rule](/docs/goes-with/goes-with-overview), or the [cart drawer upsell](/docs/upsell/cart-upsell). Two things you read the numbers on beat six you don't.
