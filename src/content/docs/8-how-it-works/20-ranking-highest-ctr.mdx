---
title: "How Highest-CTR ranking works"
description: "Ranks by click-through rate = clicks ÷ impressions, weighed by how much evidence is behind it, so an efficient product can beat a more-viewed one — but not on a handful of views."
updated: 2026-11-20
---
**Highest CTR** ranks by click-through rate — **clicks ÷ impressions** — weighed by how much evidence is behind it. It rewards efficiency, not traffic, so a product fewer people see but more of them click can lead, once it has been seen often enough to show it. Any product whose CTR works out to zero is dropped — that includes a heavily-viewed product nobody has clicked, not just unseen ones.

## 1. The collection, unranked

![The collection, unranked](/assets/ranking-highest-ctr/concept-ranking-highest-ctr-step-01.png)

A normal collection page. The order is whatever the catalog lists — the best performers could be buried on the second row where few shoppers scroll.

## 2. The ranking engine scores it

![The ranking engine scores it](/assets/ranking-highest-ctr/concept-ranking-highest-ctr-step-02.png)

The engine divides each product’s clicks by its impressions over the window, then pulls that rate toward the average CTR of the products being ranked — the average counts as 20 views, so a product seen 3 times sits close to it and one seen 4,000 times sits on its own number — and orders by the cautious low end of that estimate. One click in three views therefore doesn’t out-rank 300 in 4,000. Membership uses the plain rate: every product whose clicks ÷ impressions is 0 is dropped, whether it had no views or views and no clicks. Clicks on For You cards are counted but their views aren’t (see **Most impressions**), so a product clicked only from For You has no impressions and is dropped, and one clicked from both can read higher than its grid alone would. Engagement signals measure in whole days, so an hours-based window rounds up to whole days, and a **Period** or **Snapshot** window falls back to the last 30 days.

## 3. The front row gets discovered

![The front row gets discovered](/assets/ranking-highest-ctr/concept-ranking-highest-ctr-step-03.png)

The strongest products now fill the front row — the slots that get the most views and clicks. That’s the payoff: ranking turns your best products into the ones shoppers see first. If fewer products clear the guard than your **Max products** cap allows, the leftover slots are filled from the collection’s current order, below every product that has a real CTR.
