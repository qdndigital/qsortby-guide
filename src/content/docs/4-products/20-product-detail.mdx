Click any row on **Products** to open a single product. The metrics at the top are the same ones from the list; the five tabs underneath answer a different question: **what does this product travel with, and on what basis?**

Three of the tabs report what shoppers did. Two report what you configured. They are kept apart on purpose.

## What shoppers actually did

| Tab | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| **Bought together** | Products that appear in the same completed orders |
| **Added together** | Products added to the same cart, whether or not it converted |
| **Viewed together** | Products seen in the same browsing session |

These are observations from your own store. Nothing here is a setting, and nothing here was chosen by you — which is exactly what makes them useful when you are deciding what to configure.

## What you configured

**Pair with** shows the pairing rule this product falls under, if any — the rule that keeps a set adjacent in a sorted collection. See [Keep products side by side](/docs/collections/manage-collections).

<Callout type="note" title="Evidence and configuration stay separate">
A product set you wrote will never appear under Bought together, and co-purchase data will never quietly become a rule. If your own rule showed up as evidence, you would be reading your decision back to yourself as proof that the decision was right.
</Callout>

## Similar — and the reason for every row

**Similar** is the one tab that is inferred rather than observed: products the catalogue implies are alike. Every row carries the signals that produced it, each as a percentage:

- **Same product type** · **Same vendor** — structural matches
- **Shared tags** · **Shared collections** — how you have organised the catalogue
- **Similar price** — closeness in price band
- **Sells well** — general popularity

So a match you disagree with is never a dead end. You can see which signal produced it and go fix the data — retag the product, move it out of a collection — rather than concluding the feature is broken.

Two details worth knowing:

- **A signal that could not be compared is left out, not shown as 0%.** If neither product has tags, the tag row simply is not there. Zero would read as "no overlap" when the truth is "nothing to compare".
- **Co-purchase is reported here but kept out of the score.** If two products genuinely sell together you will see *Also bought together 12×* beside the row — labelled, and never folded into the similarity number, because "bought with" and "alike" are different questions.

</Callout>
