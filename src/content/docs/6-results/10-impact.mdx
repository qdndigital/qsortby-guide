**Results** is the fourth entry in the menu, and **Impact** is its first tab: the money question. Three more sit beside it — [Experiments](/docs/results/experiments), [Visitors](/docs/results/visitors) and [Intent](/docs/results/intent). There are no second rows of tabs and no per-card date buttons: on Impact, one range at the top of the page covers every card (Sort projects reads at least 30 days).

Impact's range is **Last 7, 30 or 90 days** — its reports count days back from today, so it offers no calendar range. **Compare** puts two periods side by side, which is the quickest way to read a before/after around the day you changed a recipe. Visitors and Intent have their own range control with custom dates; an experiment is measured over its own life, not over a range.

## What this is, honestly

Influenced revenue is **last-touch attribution over one shopping session**: when a shopper opens a product from a surface QSortby ordered, a tag is written and kept for thirty minutes; a cart add in that time carries it into the cart, and the order is credited to that surface (a *Buy it now* checkout has to complete within the thirty minutes). An upsell is credited only with the items it added, never with the whole order around them.

It measures **influence, not proven cause**. A shopper who would have bought anyway is counted the same as one who would not, and the app never uses the word "caused". What it is good for: telling you which surfaces are *involved* in sales, and how that changes when you change them. What it is not: a profit-and-loss line. For a number that controls for shoppers who would have bought anyway, run an [experiment](/docs/results/experiments).

## The sections, top to bottom

**Influenced revenue** — the headline for the range, beside **influenced orders** and the **basket difference** (influenced basket against untouched orders).

**Against the whole store** — store revenue and store orders for the same range, beside the influenced share, and the **influenced basket** next to the **untouched basket**. The comparison is against the orders QSortby did *not* touch, not against the store average — the average contains the influenced orders, so differencing against it would subtract the effect from itself.

**Where that revenue came from** — one row per surface (a sorted collection, the For You feed, the Best sellers row, each upsell touch point) with its type, influenced revenue, orders and basket. *No surface has been credited with an order in this range* is a real answer, usually on a new install or a short range.

**Influenced revenue by day** — every day of the range with its influenced orders and revenue, so a spike has a date.

**Which collections earn their clicks** — views, clicks and CTR per sorted collection (the ten most viewed), with a small CTR trend and the recipe each runs. The fastest way to see which sorts shoppers actually respond to.

**How the personalized feed did** — the For You surface on its own: shoppers served, views, click-through and revenue, with views, clicks, CTR and add-to-cart rate by device.

**What moved** — products rising and falling on revenue over the range, with the change behind each move. It loads after the rest of the page, because it answers a question that belongs to the [Products tab](/docs/auto-sort/products-tab) as much as to Results.

**Sort projects** — what each ranking configuration earned *while it was live*: orders, revenue, CTR and revenue per 1,000 views per version, so a sort you have since changed is still judged on its own window. When a week goes unusually well or badly, this is how you tell which rule was running.

## Export

**Export CSV** sits at the top of the page (the influenced series, day by day) and under the personalized feed, What moved and Sort projects, with the same range and the same definitions as the screen — so a figure in your spreadsheet means what the same figure meant on the page.

## When the pixel is down

Views, clicks and CTR come from the storefront pixel; influenced revenue is counted from orders. If the theme app embed stops reporting, **Home** marks the pixel-fed figures **frozen** rather than letting them go silently stale, with a note that influenced revenue is unaffected; on Impact the collections card says no views were recorded and points you to tracking. Auto-sort keeps running from your orders in the meantime; it is measurement that stops, not sorting.
