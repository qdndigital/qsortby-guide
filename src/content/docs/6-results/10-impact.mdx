**Results** is the fourth entry in the menu, and **Impact** is its first tab: the money question. Three more sit beside it — [Experiments](/docs/results/experiments), [Visitors](/docs/results/visitors) and [Intent](/docs/results/intent) — and all four read the **same range**, chosen once at the top of the page. There are no second rows of tabs and no per-card date buttons, so two figures side by side always cover the same period.

The range control also does **custom ranges** and a **side-by-side comparison** of two periods, which is the quickest way to read a before/after around the day you changed a recipe.

## What this is, honestly

Influenced revenue is **last-touch attribution over one shopping session**: when a shopper opens a product from a surface QSortby ordered, a tag is written; it expires after thirty minutes; an order placed while it is live is credited to that surface. An upsell is credited only with the items it added, never with the whole order around them.

It measures **influence, not proven cause**. A shopper who would have bought anyway is counted the same as one who would not, and the app never uses the word "caused". What it is good for: telling you which surfaces are *involved* in sales, and how that changes when you change them. What it is not: a profit-and-loss line. For a number that controls for shoppers who would have bought anyway, run an [experiment](/docs/results/experiments).

## The sections, top to bottom

**Influenced revenue** — the headline for the range, with the **daily series** underneath so a spike has a date.

**Against the whole store** — store revenue and store orders for the same range, beside the influenced share, and the **influenced basket** next to the **untouched basket**. The comparison is against the orders QSortby did *not* touch, not against the store average — the average contains the influenced orders, so differencing against it would subtract the effect from itself.

**Where that revenue came from** — one row per surface (a sorted collection, the For You feed, the Best sellers row, each upsell touch point) with its type, influenced revenue, orders and basket. *No surface has been credited with an order in this range* is a real answer, usually on a new install or a short range.

**Which collections earn their clicks** — impressions, clicks and CTR per sorted collection, with a small CTR trend and the recipe each runs. The fastest way to see which sorts shoppers actually respond to.

**How the personalized feed did** (Pro) — the For You surface on its own: shoppers served, click-through, cart adds, orders and revenue, split by device and by experiment arm where one is running.

**What moved** — products rising and falling on revenue over the range, with the change behind each move. It loads after the rest of the page, because it answers a question that belongs to the [Products tab](/docs/auto-sort/products-tab) as much as to Results.

**Sort projects** (Pro) — what each ranking configuration earned *while it was live*: attributed orders, revenue, basket and feed CTR per version, so a sort you have since changed is still judged on its own window. When a week goes unusually well or badly, this is how you tell which rule was running.

## Export

Each table has **Export CSV** (Pro), with the same range and the same definitions as the screen — so a figure in your spreadsheet means what the same figure meant on the page.

## When the pixel is down

Impressions, clicks and CTR come from the storefront pixel; influenced revenue is counted from orders. If the theme app embed stops reporting, the pixel-fed figures are marked **frozen** rather than silently going stale, and the revenue line carries a note saying it is unaffected. Auto-sort keeps running from your orders in the meantime; it is measurement that stops, not sorting.
