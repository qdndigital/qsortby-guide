Influenced revenue tells you a surface was involved in sales. An **experiment** is how you find out whether it *made* sales: half your visitors see one ranking, half see another, and the test measures which arm bought more. A visitor always stays in the same arm, so a returning shopper is never shown two different stores.

**Results → Experiments** lists what is running and what has finished. Free runs them without a cap; **Starter** runs one at a time; Pro and Growth are uncapped again (see [Plan limits](/docs/getting-started/plans-and-limits)).

## What an experiment can compare

Three comparison types, named after the question you actually have:

| Type | The question | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Two recipes** | Does this recipe sell more than that one on this surface? | Best selling against Trending now |
| **Two settings of one recipe** | Does the window matter? | A 7-day window against a 30-day one |
| **Personalized vs plain** | Does the published personalization rule beat the recipe order? | For You against Best sellers, on the same slots |

A fourth runs from the Intent tab rather than here: the **Intent AI on/off test**, which switches intent off for half your shoppers on For You and every offer that ranks by intent. It uses the same machinery and reports in the same place. See [Intent](/docs/results/intent).

## Setting one up

**New experiment** asks for the comparison type, the surface (a collection, the For You feed or the Best sellers row), the two arms, the **split**, and a **length**. The length is set before the test starts and the test **stops automatically** when it is reached; orders are counted until two days after the end, so a shopper exposed on the last day still has time to buy. Nothing on your storefront changes when a test ends: *the setting this test compared is changed where it lives* — on the collection or the rule — so the test can never flip your store on its own.

<Callout type="warning" title="A running test is not measuring anything yet">
Until both arms have reached the **minimum per arm** — 300 visitors — the page shows progress, not an answer, and says so. The minimum stays on screen whether or not you have reached it, so nobody adopts a winner from forty impressions.
</Callout>

## Reading the result

Each arm reports **shoppers**, **bought** (placed at least one order, anywhere in your store), **conversion** (orders per visitor), **revenue per shopper**, and the storefront funnel behind them — impressions, clicks, click-through, add-to-cart rate. Shoppers are counted **once each**, in the arm they were assigned, however many times they were served.

The verdict is a **two-proportion test** and needs **95%** before the page will call one arm ahead; below that, *the difference could be chance*, and it says so. Even above it the word is **directional**, never *proven* — a two-week test on one store is evidence, not a law — and when there is no clear difference the result says how large a difference the test *could* have seen, so "no effect" and "too small to see" are never confused.

A **Came for** breakdown splits buyers by the intent they were read as, where Intent AI is on, so you can see whether a winning arm won with gift-buyers or with everyone.

## Stopping and deleting

**Stop** ends a test early and reads it as it stands — labelled as stopped, with whatever sample it reached. **Delete** removes a finished or never-started test from the list; a running one has to be stopped first. Finished tests stay listed with their verdict so a decision made in March can still be explained in June.
