**Longest dwell** ranks by **average time on the product page** — total dwell divided by the number of views that actually reported a dwell time. Long dwell means shoppers are studying it: a considered purchase. It surfaces the items that hold interest. Products with no timed views are dropped.

## 1. The collection, unranked

![The collection, unranked](/assets/ranking-longest-dwell/concept-ranking-longest-dwell-step-01.png)

A normal collection page. The order is whatever the catalog lists — the best performers could be buried on the second row where few shoppers scroll.

## 2. The ranking engine scores it

![The ranking engine scores it](/assets/ranking-longest-dwell/concept-ranking-longest-dwell-step-02.png)

The engine divides each product’s total dwell time by the number of views that reported one — views the pixel never timed don’t dilute the average, and a single view counts for at most 10 minutes. A product seen by thousands but skimmed in seconds ranks below one that fewer shoppers study for longer — attention, not traffic. Engagement signals measure in whole days, so an hours-based window counts as one day, and a **Period** or **Snapshot** window falls back to the last 30 days.

## 3. The front row gets discovered

![The front row gets discovered](/assets/ranking-longest-dwell/concept-ranking-longest-dwell-step-03.png)

The strongest products now fill the front row — the slots that get the most views and clicks. That’s the payoff: ranking turns your best products into the ones shoppers see first. If fewer products have a timed view than your **Max products** cap allows, the leftover slots are filled from the collection’s current order, below everything with a real dwell time.
