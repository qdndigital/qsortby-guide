**Most added to cart** ranks by the **sum of add-to-cart events** in the window. An add is a stronger signal than a view or a click — a shopper saying “I want this.” Products with no add-to-carts in the window are left out rather than padding the bottom.

## 1. The collection, unranked

![The collection, unranked](/assets/ranking-most-added-to-cart/concept-ranking-most-added-to-cart-step-01.png)

A normal collection page. The order is whatever the catalog lists — the best performers could be buried on the second row where few shoppers scroll.

## 2. The ranking engine scores it

![The ranking engine scores it](/assets/ranking-most-added-to-cart/concept-ranking-most-added-to-cart-step-02.png)

The engine sums each product’s add-to-cart events over the window. Views and clicks matter less here; the deliberate act of adding to cart is real purchase intent, one step from checkout — so the most-added product takes #1. Engagement signals measure in whole days, so an hours-based window counts as one day, and a **Period** or **Snapshot** window falls back to the last 30 days.

## 3. The front row gets discovered

![The front row gets discovered](/assets/ranking-most-added-to-cart/concept-ranking-most-added-to-cart-step-03.png)

The strongest products now fill the front row — the slots that get the most views and clicks. That’s the payoff: ranking turns your best products into the ones shoppers see first. If fewer products have add-to-carts than your **Max products** cap allows, the leftover slots are filled from the collection’s current order, below every product with real intent behind it.
