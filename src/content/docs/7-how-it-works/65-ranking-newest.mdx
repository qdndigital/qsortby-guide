**New arrivals** ranks by **go-live date**, newest first. It also uses the window as a filter: products that went live *within* the window are the ones that qualify, so the collection refreshes itself as items age out. If fewer products qualify than your **Max products** cap allows, the app tops the collection up with older arrivals rather than leaving slots empty.

## 1. The collection, unranked

![The collection, unranked](/assets/ranking-newest/concept-ranking-newest-step-01.png)

A normal collection page. The order is whatever the catalog lists — the best performers could be buried on the second row where few shoppers scroll.

## 2. The ranking engine scores it

![The ranking engine scores it](/assets/ranking-newest/concept-ranking-newest-step-02.png)

The engine sorts by each product’s **live date**, most-recent first, and puts the ones whose live date falls inside the window on top. There’s no behaviour involved — it’s purely “what’s new” — and a product with no live date on record can’t be ranked at all. Anything outside the window is used only as filler below the genuine new arrivals, so lower your **Max products** cap if you want the collection to hold nothing but in-window launches.

## 3. The front row gets discovered

![The front row gets discovered](/assets/ranking-newest/concept-ranking-newest-step-03.png)

The strongest products now fill the front row — the slots that get the most views and clicks. That’s the payoff: ranking turns your best products into the ones shoppers see first.
