Ranking signals decide the *order of products*; **segment matching** decides *which order each shopper gets*. A personalization rule is an ordered list of shopper groups — each with an **audience** (who qualifies) and a **ranking** (what they see). Every visitor is checked top to bottom, and the **first group they match wins**. Here's how one shopper is routed.

## 1. A rule is an ordered list of groups

![A rule is an ordered list of groups](/assets/segment-matching/concept-segment-matching-step-01.png)

Three groups: **VIP shoppers** (2+ past purchases), **First-time visitors** (0 past purchases), and a locked **Everyone else** catch-all. Order matters — the group nearer the top is checked first. Each group carries its own signal ranking.

## 2. A shopper arrives

![A shopper arrives](/assets/segment-matching/concept-segment-matching-step-02.png)

This returning shopper has **3 past purchases**, recently viewed running shoes, and came from an email link. Each visitor is tested against every group's audience, beginning with the first.

## 3. First match wins

![First match wins](/assets/segment-matching/concept-segment-matching-step-03.png)

3 past purchases satisfies **VIP shoppers** (2+), so the shopper is placed in that group and checking **stops immediately** — the groups below are never evaluated. This is why group order matters: put your most specific audiences near the top.

## 4. They see that group's order

![They see that group's order](/assets/segment-matching/concept-segment-matching-step-04.png)

The shopper now sees the **VIP** ranking — Recently viewed first, then Top sellers, then New arrivals. A **first-time visitor** would fail the VIP audience, fall through to their own group, and see a completely different order. One rule, tailored to each shopper.
