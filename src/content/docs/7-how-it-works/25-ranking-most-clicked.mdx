**Most clicked to PDP** ranks by the **sum of clicks** through to the product page in the window. A click is the first thing a shopper *chooses* to do — it says the tile earned the trip. Unlike CTR, this is volume, not efficiency: a widely-clicked product leads even if plenty of people scrolled past it. Products with no clicks in the window are left out.

## 1. The collection, unranked

![The collection, unranked](/assets/ranking-most-clicked/concept-ranking-most-clicked-step-01.png)

A normal collection page. The order is whatever the catalog lists — the best performers could be buried on the second row where few shoppers scroll.

## 2. The ranking engine scores it

![The ranking engine scores it](/assets/ranking-most-clicked/concept-ranking-most-clicked-step-02.png)

The engine sums each product’s product-page clicks from the storefront pixel over the window and sorts descending. It sits one step past **Most impressions** and one step short of **Most added to cart** — good when you want the tiles shoppers actually open at the front, without letting a low-traffic product with a freak rate jump the queue. Engagement signals measure in whole days, so an hours-based window counts as one day, and a **Period** or **Snapshot** window falls back to the last 30 days.

## 3. The front row gets discovered

![The front row gets discovered](/assets/ranking-most-clicked/concept-ranking-most-clicked-step-03.png)

The strongest products now fill the front row — the slots that get the most views and clicks. That’s the payoff: ranking turns your best products into the ones shoppers see first. If fewer products have clicks than your **Max products** cap allows, the leftover slots are filled from the collection’s current order, below everything that was actually clicked.
