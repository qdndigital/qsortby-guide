**Best converting** ranks by **conversion rate** — purchases ÷ impressions. It rewards products that close rather than products that get looked at, so a niche item bought by nearly everyone who sees it can out-rank a crowd favorite. Any product whose conversion rate works out to zero is dropped — that includes a heavily-viewed product nobody has bought.

## 1. The collection, unranked

![The collection, unranked](/assets/ranking-best-converting/concept-ranking-best-converting-step-01.png)

A normal collection page. The order is whatever the catalog lists — the best performers could be buried on the second row where few shoppers scroll.

## 2. The ranking engine scores it

![The ranking engine scores it](/assets/ranking-best-converting/concept-ranking-best-converting-step-02.png)

The engine divides each product’s purchases by its impressions over the window — a raw rate with no smoothing, so a product seen 40 times and bought 4 beats one seen 4,000 times and bought 200. “Purchases” counts checkouts containing the product rather than units. Engagement signals measure in whole days, so an hours-based window counts as one day, and a **Period** or **Snapshot** window falls back to the last 30 days.

## 3. The front row gets discovered

![The front row gets discovered](/assets/ranking-best-converting/concept-ranking-best-converting-step-03.png)

The strongest products now fill the front row — the slots that get the most views and clicks. That’s the payoff: ranking turns your best products into the ones shoppers see first. If fewer products have converted than your **Max products** cap allows, the leftover slots are filled from the collection’s current order, below every product with a real conversion rate.
