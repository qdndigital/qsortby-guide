**Most impressions** ranks by raw reach — the **sum of impressions** in the window. It’s an awareness signal: most-seen first, regardless of how well they convert. Products with no impressions in the window are left out.

## 1. The collection, unranked

![The collection, unranked](/assets/ranking-most-impressions/concept-ranking-most-impressions-step-01.png)

A normal collection page. The order is whatever the catalog lists — the best performers could be buried on the second row where few shoppers scroll.

## 2. The ranking engine scores it

![The ranking engine scores it](/assets/ranking-most-impressions/concept-ranking-most-impressions-step-02.png)

The engine sums each product’s impressions from the storefront pixel over the window. A product that converts brilliantly but is rarely seen still ranks below a widely-viewed one — reach, not conversion, sets this order. Engagement signals measure in whole days, so an hours-based window counts as one day, and a **Period** or **Snapshot** window falls back to the last 30 days.

## 3. The front row gets discovered

![The front row gets discovered](/assets/ranking-most-impressions/concept-ranking-most-impressions-step-03.png)

The strongest products now fill the front row — the slots that get the most views and clicks. That’s the payoff: ranking turns your best products into the ones shoppers see first. If fewer products have impressions than your **Max products** cap allows, the leftover slots are filled from the collection’s current order, below everything that was actually seen.
