**Highest CTR** ranks by click-through rate — **clicks ÷ impressions** — a raw ratio with no smoothing. It rewards efficiency, not traffic, so a product few people see but many click can lead. Any product whose CTR works out to zero is dropped — that includes a heavily-viewed product nobody has clicked, not just unseen ones.

## 1. The collection, unranked

![The collection, unranked](/assets/ranking-highest-ctr/concept-ranking-highest-ctr-step-01.png)

A normal collection page. The order is whatever the catalog lists — the best performers could be buried on the second row where few shoppers scroll.

## 2. The ranking engine scores it

![The ranking engine scores it](/assets/ranking-highest-ctr/concept-ranking-highest-ctr-step-02.png)

The engine divides each product’s clicks by its impressions over the window. There’s no Bayesian/Wilson smoothing — it’s the literal rate — with a single guard that drops every product scoring 0, whether that’s because it had no views or because it had views and no clicks. The highest rate takes #1, even if it’s seen the least. Engagement signals measure in whole days, so an hours-based window counts as one day, and a **Period** or **Snapshot** window falls back to the last 30 days.

## 3. The front row gets discovered

![The front row gets discovered](/assets/ranking-highest-ctr/concept-ranking-highest-ctr-step-03.png)

The strongest products now fill the front row — the slots that get the most views and clicks. That’s the payoff: ranking turns your best products into the ones shoppers see first. If fewer products clear the guard than your **Max products** cap allows, the leftover slots are filled from the collection’s current order, below every product that has a real CTR.
