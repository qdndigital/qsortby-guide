A sort rule is built from three choices on the Setup tab: which signal ranks your products, how far back the sales window looks, and how often qSortBy re-sorts. This guide explains all three so you can dial in exactly the ordering you want.

## 1. Pick the ranking signal

![Pick the ranking signal](/assets/sort-signals/qsortby-sort-signals-step-01.png)

Left menu → **Collections** → open a collection → the **Setup** tab. Under **Sort products by**, choose one of seven signals: **Best sellers** (units sold), **Highest CTR** (click-through rate), **Highest revenue** (sales value), **Newest** (recently added), **Most inventory** (stock on hand), **Trending** (recent momentum), or **Manual pinning** (a hand-set order). Each ranks the same products differently.

<Callout type="tip" title="Tip">
Use Trending to surface fast-rising products, or Best sellers for a steadier, proven order.
</Callout>

## 2. Set the sales window

![Set the sales window](/assets/sort-signals/qsortby-sort-signals-step-02.png)

The **Use sales from** window controls how far back qSortBy looks when measuring a signal. A short window reacts quickly to what's selling right now; a longer window smooths out day-to-day swings and favours consistent performers.

## 3. Choose the update cadence

![Choose the update cadence](/assets/sort-signals/qsortby-sort-signals-step-03.png)

Decide how often the collection re-sorts. Continuous updates re-rank as sales come in for the freshest order, while an hourly or daily cadence gives a steadier storefront that changes less often. Pick the rhythm that suits your catalogue.

## 4. Confirm it in plain words

![Confirm it in plain words](/assets/sort-signals/qsortby-sort-signals-step-04.png)

The **In plain words** panel restates your signal, window, and cadence as one readable sentence — a quick way to confirm the rule does what you intend before you save it.

Signal, sales window, and cadence together define how a collection sorts. Adjust any of the three and the In plain words summary updates so you always know exactly what qSortBy will do.
